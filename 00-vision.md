# RemitRight LA — Project Vision

## 1. What are you proposing, and for whom?

We propose RemitRight LA, a notebook-based decision-support tool for a campaign researcher at the Coalition for Humane Immigrant Rights (CHIRLA). The same core tool could be adapted for an IFAD program officer selecting corridors for a cost-reduction program.

The researcher must decide which U.S.-to-destination corridor should receive CHIRLA's next "know what you really pay" campaign. This decision would be made annually when outreach budgets are set and reviewed when new price data arrives.

The researcher would select periods, one transfer amount, destination countries, and scoring weights in clearly labeled notebook cells. The output would show median total cost, visible fees, exchange-rate margins, non-transparent services, and differences between internet and counter quotes.

A trend chart would show whether costs and transparency improved, while a provider table would flag unknown margins. After comparing a shortlist, the researcher could export a briefing that helps CHIRLA choose a community and campaign message.

## 2. Why is it relevant?

Remittance costs reduce the money reaching families, yet the full price is difficult to understand because it combines a visible fee with an exchange-rate margin.

CHIRLA needs to direct limited outreach resources toward a community facing a persistent, explainable price problem. Choosing only the corridor with the highest cost could misdirect the campaign if few local members use it, the result depends on one unusual quote, or providers did not disclose their exchange rates.

CHIRLA could review World Bank corridor pages, spreadsheets, and community feedback separately, but that process is slow and inconsistent.

RemitRight LA would combine cost persistence, service channels, and transparency in one reproducible report while leaving priorities under staff control. U.S.-origin price quotes do not establish local community relevance; that requires separate evidence or researcher input.

Key concerns are staff time, data freshness, member privacy, and the risk of treating survey quotes as live prices. The tool would show collection dates, explain each score, use no personal member records, and remind staff to verify current prices before publishing.

## 3. What do users care about?

The campaign researcher needs reliable, understandable, and reproducible information to support outreach decisions.

The main priorities are:

- **Cost:** Identify corridors with high median total transfer costs and assess whether those costs persist over time, including visible fees and exchange-rate margins.
- **Transparency:** Identify services with missing exchange-rate information or non-transparent pricing.
- **Trends:** Understand whether transfer costs and price transparency have improved over time.
- **Relevance:** Consider whether a corridor matters to the communities CHIRLA serves, rather than relying on high costs alone.
- **Explainability:** Understand how scoring weights affect rankings and explain the evidence behind each recommendation.
- **Practicality:** Produce useful results without extensive technical work, personal member records, or live pricing data.

## 4. Use cases

### Use Case 1: Compare remittance corridors

The researcher selects one reporting period, one transfer amount, and destination countries.

RemitRight LA compares median total transfer costs across the selected corridors using the same transfer amount. The researcher uses these results to identify high-cost corridors for further review.

### Use Case 2: Evaluate pricing transparency

The researcher selects periods, one transfer amount, and destination countries, then examines visible fees, exchange-rate margins, non-transparent service shares, and differences between internet and counter quotes.

The provider table highlights unknown margins so the researcher can identify pricing and disclosure issues to explain in the campaign.

### Use Case 3: Review trends and campaign priorities

The researcher selects multiple periods, destination countries, one transfer amount, and scoring weights.

RemitRight LA shows cost and transparency trends alongside the corridor shortlist. Where the required evidence is available, scores support comparison; unavailable scores are explained.

The researcher uses the evidence to assess persistent problems and decide which corridor warrants further investigation, considering community relevance separately from price data.

### Use Case 4: Export a campaign briefing

After reviewing the results, the researcher exports a briefing containing the selected settings, supporting tables and charts, source information, and limitations.

The briefing supports staff discussion about which community to prioritize and which campaign message to develop.
