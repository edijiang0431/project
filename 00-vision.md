# RemitRight LA — Project Vision

## 1. What are you proposing, and for whom?

I propose RemitRight LA, a notebook-based decision-support tool
for a campaign researcher at the Coalition for Humane Immigrant
Rights (CHIRLA). The same core tool could be adapted for an IFAD
program officer selecting corridors for a cost-reduction program.

The researcher must decide which U.S.-to-destination corridor
should receive CHIRLA's next "know what you really pay" campaign.
This decision would be made annually when outreach budgets are
set and reviewed when new price data arrives.

The researcher would enter a period, transfer amount, destination
countries, and scoring weights in clearly labeled notebook cells.
The output would show median total cost, visible fees, exchange-rate
margins, non-transparent services, and differences between internet
and counter quotes.

A trend chart would reveal whether costs and transparency improved,
while a provider table would flag unknown margins. After comparing
a shortlist, the researcher could export a briefing that helps
CHIRLA choose a community and campaign message.

## 2. Why is it relevant?

Remittance costs reduce the money reaching families, yet the full
price is difficult to understand because it combines a visible fee
with an exchange-rate margin.

CHIRLA needs to direct limited outreach resources toward a community
facing a persistent, explainable price problem. Choosing only the
corridor with the highest average could misdirect the campaign if
few local members use it, the result depends on one unusual quote,
or providers did not disclose their exchange rates.

CHIRLA could review World Bank corridor pages, spreadsheets, and
community feedback separately, but that process is slow and
inconsistent.

RemitRight LA would combine cost persistence, service channels,
and transparency in one reproducible report while leaving priorities
under staff control.

Key concerns are staff time, data freshness, member privacy, and
the risk of treating survey quotes as live prices. The tool would
show collection dates, explain each score, use no personal records,
and remind staff to verify current prices before publishing.

## 3. What do users care about?

The campaign researcher needs reliable, understandable, and
reproducible information to support outreach decisions.

The main priorities are:

- **Cost:** Identify corridors with consistently high median total
  transfer costs, including visible fees and exchange-rate margins.

- **Transparency:** Identify providers with missing exchange-rate
  information or other non-transparent pricing practices.

- **Trends:** Understand whether transfer costs and price
  transparency have improved over time.

- **Relevance:** Consider whether a corridor is relevant to the
  communities CHIRLA serves, rather than relying on high costs alone.

- **Explainability:** Understand how scoring weights affect rankings
  and explain the evidence behind each recommendation.

- **Practicality:** Produce useful results without extensive technical
  work, personal member records, or live pricing data.

## 4. Use Cases

### Use Case 1: Compare remittance corridors

The researcher selects a reporting period, transfer amount, and
destination countries.

RemitRight LA compares median total transfer costs across the
selected corridors, helping the researcher identify corridors
with persistently high costs.
